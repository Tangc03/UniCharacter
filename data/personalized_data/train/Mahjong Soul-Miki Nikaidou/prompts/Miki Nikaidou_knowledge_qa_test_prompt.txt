### 1. The Fundamentals of Riichi Mahjong

Mahjong Soul is based on **Riichi Mahjong** (also known as Japanese Modern Mahjong), a popular variant of the game. Here are its fundamental principles:

* **The Goal:** The ultimate goal is to be the first player to complete a legal winning hand and to finish the game with more points than any other player.
* **The Hand:** A standard winning hand consists of 14 tiles, divided into **four sets (melds)** and **one pair**.
    * **Sets (Melds):** These can be either a *Koutsu* (triplet, three identical tiles) or a *Shuntsu* (sequence, three consecutive tiles in the same suit).
    * **Pair (Atama):** Two identical tiles.
* **Yaku (Winning Patterns):** Unlike some other mahjong variants, you cannot win a hand just by having four sets and a pair. Your hand *must* contain at least one *yaku* (a specific scoring pattern). Examples of yaku include:
    * *Riichi:* Declaring a ready hand (explained below).
    * *Tanyao:* A hand with no terminal (1 or 9) or honor (wind/dragon) tiles.
    * *Pinfu:* A no-points hand made of all sequences, a non-scoring pair, and an open-ended wait.
    * *Yakuhai:* A triplet of dragon tiles or the prevailing/seat wind.
* **Riichi (Ready Hand):** This is the game's signature rule. If you have a *tenpai* (ready) hand—meaning you are one tile away from winning—and your hand is *menzenchin* (fully concealed, no "called" melds), you can declare "Riichi." You discard a tile sideways and wager 1,000 points.
    * **Benefit:** Winning after declaring Riichi grants you the *Riichi* yaku, adds one *dora* (bonus tile) via the *ura-dora*, and often increases the hand's value significantly.
    * **Risk:** You can no longer change your hand. You must automatically discard any tile you draw that does not complete your hand (unless it can be used to form a concealed *kan*).
* **Dora (Bonus Tiles):** These tiles add *han* (double value) to a winning hand, but they *do not* count as yaku. You still need a separate yaku to win. The dora is indicated by the tile flipped over on the dead wall.
* **Fuurou (Calling Tiles):** You can "call" an opponent's discard to complete a meld, but this opens your hand.
    * **Chii:** Calling to complete a sequence (can only be done from the player to your left).
    * **Pon:** Calling to complete a triplet (can be done from any player).
    * **Kan:** Calling to complete a quad (can be done from any player).
    * The main drawback is that opening your hand disqualifies you from using *Riichi* and many other valuable concealed-hand yaku, often resulting in a cheaper, faster hand.
