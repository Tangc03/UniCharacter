### Part 1: The Fundamentals of Riichi Mahjong

Riichi Mahjong, the variant of mahjong featured in Mahjong Soul, is a game of skill, strategy, and a touch of luck. The primary objective is to be the first of four players to form a winning hand of 14 tiles, typically consisting of four sets of three and a pair.

**The Basics of Gameplay:**

- **Tiles:** The game is played with a set of 136 tiles, divided into three suits (Manzu/Characters, Pinzu/Circles, and Souzu/Bamboo), numbered 1 through 9, and honor tiles (Winds and Dragons).
- **Drawing and Discarding:** Each player starts with 13 tiles. On their turn, a player draws a tile from the wall and must then discard one tile from their hand. This core mechanic of drawing and discarding continues in a counter-clockwise rotation.
- **Forming Sets (Mentsu):** A winning hand is composed of four "mentsu" (sets) and a "jantou" (pair). There are three types of sets:
  - **Chii (or Chow):** A sequence of three consecutive tiles of the same suit (e.g., 3, 4, 5 of Circles). A player can only call "chii" on a discard from the player to their immediate left.
  - **Pon (or Pung):** A triplet of three identical tiles (e.g., three Red Dragons). A player can call "pon" on a discard from any other player.
  - **Kan (or Kong):** A quadruplet of four identical tiles. Calling "kan" allows a player to draw an extra tile from the dead wall.

**The Importance of "Yaku":**
A crucial element of Riichi Mahjong is the concept of "yaku," which are specific patterns or conditions that a winning hand must meet to be valid. Simply having four sets and a pair is not enough to win. There are numerous yaku, ranging from simple ones like having a triplet of Dragon tiles to more complex and high-scoring ones.

**Declaring "Riichi":**
When a player is one tile away from a winning hand and their hand is "closed" (meaning they have not called "chii" or "pon"), they can declare "Riichi." This involves betting 1,000 points and placing their discard sideways. After declaring Riichi, the player's hand is locked, and they must automatically discard any tile they draw that does not complete their hand. A successful win after declaring Riichi grants an additional yaku and potentially reveals more "dora" (bonus tiles) for a higher score.

**Winning and Scoring:**
A player can win in two ways:

- **Tsumo:** Winning by drawing the final tile themselves from the wall.
- **Ron:** Winning by claiming another player's discarded tile to complete their hand.
  The value of a winning hand is calculated based on two factors: "han" (the number of yaku in the hand) and "fu" (a more complex calculation based on the hand's composition and how it was won). These values are then used to determine the point exchange between players.
