### 1. The Fundamentals of Riichi Mahjong

*Mahjong Soul* is based on Japanese Riichi Mahjong, a four-player game that combines skill, strategy, and a degree of luck. The ultimate goal is to finish the game with the most points by completing a winning hand of 14 tiles.

**Game Setup and Tiles:**
The game is played with a set of 136 tiles, which are divided into three suits and honor tiles.
*   **Suits:** These include Characters (Manzu), Circles (Pinzu), and Bamboo (Souzu), each numbered 1 through 9.
*   **Honors:** These are split into Wind tiles (East, South, West, North) and Dragon tiles (White, Green, Red).
*   **Red Fives (Aka Dora):** Some sets include special red-colored "5" tiles from each suit that act as bonus tiles to increase a hand's value.

Each player starts with 13 tiles, and the dealer begins with 14. The remaining tiles form a central "wall" from which players draw.

**Gameplay and Turns:**
Players take turns in anti-clockwise order. A turn consists of drawing one tile from the wall and then discarding one tile from your hand into the center of the table. The objective is to form a winning hand, which typically consists of four sets of three tiles (melds) and one pair.

**Melds (Calling Tiles):**
Players can complete their melds by using a tile discarded by another player. This is called "melding" or "calling."
*   **Chii:** Calling a discarded tile from the player to your left to complete a sequence (e.g., 5-6-7 of the same suit).
*   **Pon:** Calling a discarded tile from any player to complete a triplet (three identical tiles).
*   **Kan:** Calling a tile to form a quad (four identical tiles).
When a player makes a call, they must reveal the completed meld. A hand with one or more revealed melds is considered "open."

**Winning the Hand (Yaku and Riichi):**
To legally win a round, a player's hand must contain at least one *yaku*, which is a specific winning pattern or condition. This is a key feature of Riichi Mahjong that prevents players from winning with just any combination of melds and a pair.

A crucial yaku is **Riichi**. A player can declare "Riichi" when their hand is concealed (no open melds) and is just one tile away from completion (*tenpai*). To do this, they place a 1,000-point stick on the table and discard a tile sideways. After declaring Riichi, their hand is locked, and they must automatically discard any tile they draw that doesn't complete their hand. Declaring Riichi is a high-risk, high-reward strategy that can significantly increase a hand's value if won.

**Scoring:**
Scoring in Riichi Mahjong is complex, combining two factors:
*   **Han:** The value derived from the yaku and any bonus "Dora" tiles in the hand. Dora are indicator tiles that add value but do not count as a yaku on their own.
*   **Fu:** "Minipoints" awarded based on the composition of the hand, such as the types of melds, the winning tile wait, and how the hand was won.
